# Directory Submissions & Citation Building

Filling in forms on other sites *is* a real ranking tactic. It's called **citation
building**. But the value comes from a small number of good listings filled in
*consistently* — not from volume.

---

## The one rule that matters

Every listing must contain your **NAP** — Name, Address, Phone — written **identically,
character for character**. Google cross-references these to confirm your business is
real. Inconsistent details are the #1 reason local businesses fail to rank.

"Suite 4" vs "Ste 4". "+92 300 1234567" vs "0300-1234567". Those count as different
businesses to a crawler.

## Tier 1 — do these first (highest impact, all free)

These are worth more than every other listing combined.

| # | Site | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Google Business Profile** | Map pack + the panel that appears when someone Googles your name. Nothing else comes close. |
| 2 | **Bing Places** | Feeds Bing *and* several AI search tools. 5-minute import from Google. |
| 3 | **LinkedIn Company Page** | Ranks for your brand name almost immediately. Essential for the B2B/tech side. |
| 4 | **Facebook Page** | Still a top-3 branded search result for most local businesses. |
| 5 | **Instagram business account** | Same, and it's where non-tech clients will check you out. |
| 6 | **Apple Business Connect** | Powers Apple Maps + Siri. Free, and most competitors skip it. |

## Tier 3 — local & general (Pakistan)

Search these patterns to find current ones rather than trusting an old list:

```
"business directory" Pakistan add listing
"[your city] business directory" submit
Chamber of Commerce [your city] membership
```

Also worth doing:
- Your city's **Chamber of Commerce** — genuine local authority signal
- **Yelp** (weak in Pakistan, still indexes)
- Any **industry association** you can legitimately join
- Universities/alumni business directories if you qualify

**Verify each site is still active before submitting** — directory sites die constantly,
and half the lists circulating online are years out of date.

## ⚠️ What to avoid — this is where people damage their site

**Do not buy "500 directory submissions for $10"** on Fiverr or similar. This is the
single most common way a new site gets hurt. What you receive:

- Auto-generated listings on dead sites nobody visits
- Inconsistent NAP data (they mistype your details across hundreds of sites, and
  you can't get it corrected — this is genuinely hard to clean up)
- Links from spam networks that Google already classifies as manipulative

Also avoid:

| Tactic | Why not |
|---|---|
| Blog comment spam | Ignored since ~2012. Pure waste. |
| Forum profile links | Same. Detected and discounted. |
| Contact-form blasting | Sending mass messages through other people's contact forms is spam, gets your domain and IP blacklisted, and produces no links at all. |
| Paid links / link exchanges | Direct violation of Google's link spam policy. Manual penalty risk. |
| PBNs (private blog networks) | Highest-risk tactic there is. Sites get deindexed entirely. |

Google's SpamBrain handles most of this automatically — bad links usually get
*ignored* rather than penalised. Which means the realistic outcome of buying 500
links isn't a penalty; it's that you wasted the money and got nothing. The penalty
risk is the tail case, not the main one.

**Rule of thumb:** if a human would never plausibly visit that page, the link is worth
zero. 20 real listings beat 500 fake ones, permanently.

## What actually outranks directory work

Citations mainly help you win *branded* and *local* search. To rank for competitive
service terms, the order of impact is roughly:

1. **Google reviews** — ask every client. Direct local ranking factor, and the
   strongest conversion element you have. Reply to all of them.
2. **Case studies with real numbers** on your own site — these rank *and* close deals.
3. **Genuine editorial links** — guest posts, local press, partner sites, being
   quoted as a source. One of these outweighs 100 directories.
4. **Consistent blogging** — answering questions your clients actually ask.

## Realistic time budget

- **Tier 1: ~2 hours total.** Do it in one sitting this week. This is 80% of the value.
- **Tier 2: ~2 hours.** Do it over the following week.
- **Tier 3: 30 min/month.** Add a few, don't rush it — natural pacing looks natural.

Then stop. Citation building has a hard ceiling; once you've claimed the legitimate
listings, more submissions add nothing. Everything after that is reviews and content.
